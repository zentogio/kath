# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Style-conscious shoppers, primarily in Thailand, who care about quality over volume — people willing to pay more for a piece that's hand-cut in limited numbers rather than mass-produced. They browse with intent: they want to see the craft up close (fabric, stitching, silhouette) before deciding, then either add a piece to their cart or reach out directly about a custom order. They're used to fast-fashion marketplaces and are visiting Studio Kath specifically because it promises something slower and more considered.

## Product Purpose

Studio Kath's site is the brand's home online: a shop-style showcase of the current collection, the story behind the studio's hand-cut, small-batch approach, and a way to get in touch. Visitors can browse the collection and add pieces to a cart with a polished, considered interaction model — the cart UI is fully designed but not wired to real payment yet, since the brand isn't taking live orders through the site at this stage. Success looks like a visitor leaving with a clear sense of what Studio Kath stands for and, ideally, a reason to reach out or come back when checkout goes live.

## Positioning

Hand-cut, small-batch clothing made to last, not to trend — quality and scarcity over speed and volume.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: Add to cart / explore the collection (primary); reach out via the contact channel for custom orders or questions (secondary)
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: every piece is cut by hand, in limited numbers — this isn't fast fashion
- Belief ladder: this is genuinely hand-crafted, not marketing copy → the limited quantities are real, not a false-scarcity gimmick → the quality justifies the price → I trust this brand enough to add something to my cart or ask a question
- Proof on hand: none yet — ship with placeholder product photography and copy; revisit once real product shots and pricing exist

## Brand Personality

Quiet luxury: minimal, refined, considered. The brand doesn't shout — it lets the craft and the whitespace do the talking. Confident rather than flashy; the emotional goal is to make a visitor feel like they've found something made with care, not mass-produced. Reference points the user pointed to (tresfashion.co, starfadesinternational.com) both lean on generous whitespace, bold-but-restrained sans typography, and photography-led product presentation — that's the register to build in.

## Anti-references

Should not read as a discount marketplace (Shopee/Lazada-style): no promo banners, no coupon-code callouts, no cluttered grids competing for attention. Nothing that undercuts the "limited, hand-made" claim with mass-retail visual noise.

## Design Principles

- Let the product photography and craftsmanship carry the page — whitespace and restraint are the luxury signal, not decoration.
- Scarcity should read as real, not performative: no countdown timers or fake urgency, just clear, confident presentation of limited pieces.
- Motion and interaction should feel as considered as the clothing — smooth, deliberate, never gimmicky or janky.
- Black-and-white palette stays disciplined throughout; if an accent is ever needed, it should be rare and earn its place.
- Every page should reinforce "hand-cut, small-batch" — the story isn't confined to the About page.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific requirements were flagged, so the baseline is WCAG AA: sufficient color contrast (checked explicitly given the black-and-white palette), full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` fallback for every animation.
